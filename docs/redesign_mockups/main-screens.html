Serif for hero numbers and section titles. Sans for body and labels. Mono for all financial figures, dates, percentages, and metadata. The split is rigid by design — never break it.

Design notes · why these choices

Why dark

Light themes read as productivity software. Dark themes read as premium (Linear, Things, Arc, Bloomberg). For an app a user opens to look at their net worth — often emotional, sometimes anxious — dark also reduces visual fatigue and lets the type breathe.

Why a serif hero

Every other portfolio app sets its big number in a sans-serif. The serif here is the single biggest aesthetic differentiator. Fraunces at light weight, slightly negative tracking, optical-size 144 — it reads as confident and old-money without being stuffy.

Why amber, not blue

Blue is fintech default. Green is Robinhood. Purple is AI default. A soft warm amber (#D4A574) is unowned in this category and signals private wealth without being literal-gold. Used sparingly: chart line, active states, the brand dot, and the primary CTA only.

Why segmented bar, not donut

Donut charts are everywhere, hard to read at small sizes, and look generic. A horizontal segmented bar reads as more architectural, packs better on mobile, and lets the eye scan allocation in one second. Save the donut for the desktop view if needed.

Why mini sparklines per row

The current dashboard shows a day-change badge per position. A 12-week sparkline tells the same story plus shape and trend in roughly the same footprint. More information, less noise. Color is muted positive/negative, not red/green.

Why a journal-feeling diary

The Diary is Vesper's most differentiated feature. It should not look like a transaction list. Italic serif dates, monospace action tags, italic body for context. The "On this day" callout is the moment that makes it feel personal. Worth investing here.

Why bottom nav with 4 sections

Three tabs (Portfolio / Diary / Profile) was cramped. Adding Chat as a first-class destination makes sense given how central the assistant is. Four icons hits the sweet spot for thumb-reach on a phone, and Chat-as-tab beats Chat-as-FAB for discoverability.

Free static map · OpenFreeMap

The placeholder image uses OpenFreeMap — fully free, no API key, no rate limits, OSM-derived. Render via MapLibre GL JS with interactivity disabled, applying a dark style to match the palette. The same lat/lng powers the click-through to Google Maps street view. No paid service needed.

Architectural slots, not placeholders

The dashed "SOON" cards for WOZ, market estimates, and cash flow are not visual debt — they are load-bearing. They commit the page to its future shape so the user understands where the product is going, and so we don't have to reorganize the layout when those features land.

Real estate · property hub

The detail page is structured as a hub, not a tracker. The hero is equity, not gross property value, because that is what the net worth calculation uses. The mortgage section anchors the page. WOZ-style valuation and cash flow are the two future expansion areas, and the layout reserves room for them now.

Web is a deliberate expansion

Desktop reuses every mobile component. The expansion is structural: a left rail nav, a wider hero with a larger chart, a permanent right rail for assistant + recent activity. No new visual language — same tokens, same type, same accent. One brand, two viewports.
